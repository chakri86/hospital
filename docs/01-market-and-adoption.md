# Market validation and adoption

## Commercial hypothesis

Selected small facilities may pay for reliable clinical records and follow-up workflows when the platform reduces visible staff effort. This is a hypothesis; no customer interviews, competitor quotations or hospital commitments have been completed for this repository.

Begin with one district cluster reachable by a local implementation lead. Compare candidate locations by founder access to owners, presence of a clinical champion, travel cost, target-facility density, connectivity and ability to support Telugu-speaking staff. Hyderabad, Warangal and Vijayawada–Guntur are possible discovery areas, not validated launch recommendations.

## Discovery sample and questions

Interview 20 organizations: ten clinics, six small hospitals and four laboratories. Observe five workflows with permission and without copying real records. Include paper-based and already digitized facilities so the proposal does not assume every site has the same gap.

| Topic | Ask or measure | Decision informed |
|---|---|---|
| Current tools | Which registration, billing, clinical and lab systems are actually used? | Capture application versus connector |
| Documentation | Who records diagnosis, medication and follow-up today? | Role and workflow design |
| Volume | Visits per day, peak concurrency, documents per visit | Capacity and pricing |
| Burden | Time spent retrieving history and tracing missing reports | Demonstrable value |
| Procurement | Who signs, who pays, who can veto? | Sales process |
| Existing contracts | Export rights, API fees, renewal dates and termination conditions | Integration feasibility |
| Budget | Will the buyer sign and pay at a stated price? | Willingness to pay |
| Connectivity | Outage frequency, duration and available devices | Offline boundary |
| Support | Working hours, acceptable response time, on-site expectations | Service cost |
| Data use | What secondary uses are acceptable and contractually permitted? | Governance scope |

Capture anonymized operational notes, observer, date, baseline measurements and unresolved questions. Keep named prospect/contact information in a private business system, not this public repository.

## Competitive assessment

Assess the incumbent at each facility against clinical documentation speed, export capability, integration support, offline behavior, Telugu usability, training, total contract cost and data portability. Compare three real vendor quotations before setting a public price. Competitor prices and market share have not been verified, so no superiority claim is made here.

Where the incumbent works well, offer an integration or reporting module rather than requiring replacement. Avoid contracting directly with a hospital for an interface its incumbent vendor will not permit.

## Price experiments

| Segment | Initial experiment | Scope boundary |
|---|---:|---|
| Clinic | ₹3,000/month | Adult OPD workflow; defined usage and remote support |
| Small hospital | ₹18,000/month | Outpatient deployment; no full inpatient HIS |
| Laboratory | ₹8,000/month | Orders/results; device interfaces separately scoped |
| Standard onboarding | ₹20,000 once, weighted average | Agreed import, configuration and limited training |

These proposed prices are inputs, not prevailing market rates. Offer time-limited pilot terms with a written continuation price. Measure actual collected revenue, discounts, ticket hours and site visits. Pass-through messaging, custom integrations and expanded storage need agreed pricing; do not bill them unexpectedly.

## Adoption plan

Select a facility champion, document the current workflow, configure a minimal template and train each role on its own tasks. Run synthetic rehearsals before live use. For initial live operation, maintain an agreed downtime process and daily reconciliation against the facility's official register. Assign a single escalation channel.

Do not reward staff for inflated encounter counts or unnecessary clinical data entry. Pay implementation partners for verified go-live quality and retention, not patient recruitment or clinical referrals.

## Sales funnel and retention

Track contacted organizations → qualified buyers → demonstrations → written pilots → live pilots → paid continuations → renewals. The base financial scenario assumes gross new paying facilities, not free sign-ups. At an illustrative 20% close rate, adding 20 paying facilities in a month requires about 100 qualified opportunities; the sales capacity to do that must be proven.

For each lost customer, record whether the cause was price, workflow, reliability, incumbent contract, missing functionality or facility closure. Review adoption and service burden before raising growth targets. Publish transparent export/exit terms so trust does not depend on lock-in.

## Government and research pathways

A public-sector deployment requires separate procurement and data-sharing arrangements, eligibility review and a contract-specific operating model. Government receivables may need different collection-lag assumptions. Research customers require an approved project and independently costed delivery plan. Both revenue lines remain zero in the baseline.
